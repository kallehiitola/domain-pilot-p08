How to work out how many lumens a room needs

A room lumen budget translates floor area and how you use the space into total light output across all lamps and fixtures, factoring in light absorbed by walls and gradual LED dimming over time.

Step-by-step measuring walkthrough

  1. Measure room length and width in feet or meters to get the floor area.
  2. Pick a target light level in foot-candles (lumens per sq ft) or lux (lumens per m²) from the room reference table.
  3. Set a utilization fraction (commonly 0.65 to 0.75) for light lost to walls, furniture, and fixture housings—use a lower number in dark-painted rooms.
  4. Set a maintenance fraction (typically 0.85 to 0.90) to allow for dust and long-term lamp lumen depreciation.
  5. Divide the total installed lumen budget across your planned ceiling fixtures, sconces, and task lamps.

What to enter in the calculator

  • Room length: Room length in feet or meters.
  • Room width: Room width in feet or meters.
  • Target illuminance: Foot-candles (US) or lux (metric); see table below.
  • Utilization fraction: Share of lamp light reaching the target plane (lower for dark walls).
  • Maintenance fraction: Allows for dust and lamp ageing (typically 0.85 to 0.90 for LEDs).

Common mistakes to avoid

  • Confusing lumens (total light emitted by a bulb) with lux or foot-candles (light actually landing on a surface).
  • Shopping by wattage instead of the Lighting Facts lumen number on the bulb box.
  • Putting the entire lumen budget into one harsh overhead bulb instead of layering ambient, task, and accent lighting.
  • Using the same target brightness for a bedroom and a kitchen counter—tasks like food prep or drafting need 2× to 4× higher light levels.

Practical ordering checklist

  • Remember the exact relationship: 1 foot-candle equals 1 lumen per square foot (approx. 10.764 lux).
  • Dark walls, high ceilings, and deep shades lower utilization and raise the installed lumen budget.
  • Add dimmers when designing a room to the upper end of its brightness range.
